Key Findings
⚠️ Critical Window: The first 3 months are decisive. ~45% of young claimants exit in this period. Those who remain past 6 months have significantly higher long-term persistence.
📊 Age Paradox: 18-19 year olds have the highest persistence rates despite being in "Searching for Work" conditionality. Lack of work experience is a key factor.
🏥 Health Conditions: Those entering with LCWRA (Limited Capability for Work Related Activity) have 85%+ persistence at 24 months, reflecting chronic conditions.
💡 Policy Implication: Early, intensive support in the first 3-6 months is crucial. Once past 12 months, exit rates decline sharply.

🎯 The Compelling Statistics (LCWRA-specific)
A young person assessed as LCWRA at 18 is more likely to still be claiming at 30 than to have entered sustained work.

The calculation: IFS data shows UC health claimants have ~3%/year exit rate to employment. At 3% annual exit: 0.97^12 = 69% still claiming after 12 years.

This applies specifically to LCWRA (Limited Capability for Work-Related Activity) claimants - 66% of all WCA decisions result in LCWRA, and 69% have mental/behavioural disorders.

📋 Data Sources & Methodology
⚠️ Key distinction: LCWRA vs general UC

The "more likely to reach 30 claiming" statistic applies to LCWRA claimants specifically, not all UC claimants. General UC claimants have higher exit rates (~45% leave within 3 months).

Data sources for these calculations:

  • 3% annual exit rate: IFS analysis of UC health claimant flows (OBR Welfare Trends Report Oct 2024)
  • 66% LCWRA rate: DWP WCA statistics Jan 2022-May 2024
  • 900k 10yr+ IB claimants: PMC study on sickness benefit trends 1995-2014
  • 69% mental health: DWP WCA decision data

Key assumptions:

  • Exit rates decline exponentially with duration (duration dependence)
  • Young claimants show higher persistence than older claimants at same duration
  • LCWRA claimants have near-zero exit rates
  • Cohort effects (e.g. COVID entrants) may differ from averages

For robust estimates, DWP should publish official cohort survival analysis from the UC Longitudinal Dataset.

Prediction Model Methodology

Model Structure

The projection model uses a cohort-flow approach:

  • SEND school leavers → NEET transition (~25% rate)
  • Child DLA recipients → Adult inactivity (~40%)
  • Youth mental health trends (exponential growth)
  • Labour market demand (sector employment)
  • Policy effects (Get Britain Working offset)

Key Assumptions

  • SEND population continues ~4% annual growth
  • Child DLA claims continue ~7% annual growth
  • Entry-level jobs decline 2-4% annually
  • AI displacement begins affecting 1-2% of entry roles by 2028
  • Mental health inactivity growth slows from 12% to 8% by 2030

Limitations

Model does not account for: major economic shocks, significant policy changes beyond current announcements, demographic shifts, or immigration effects.
