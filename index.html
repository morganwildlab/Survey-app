Ben Baumberg Geiger's Methodological Considerations

Professor Ben Baumberg Geiger at King's College London has identified several issues with how official welfare statistics are presented. This tool implements adjustments based on his analysis.

1. UC vs Legacy Benefit Counting

Universal Credit counts partners in a household as separate individuals in the incapacity benefits caseload. Legacy ESA only counted individual claimants. This means UC caseloads appear artificially inflated compared to historical ESA data.

Adjustment: Apply a reduction factor (0.85) to UC caseload figures.

2. LCW 'No Payment' Claimants

Since 2017, claimants with Limited Capability for Work (LCW) but not LCWRA receive no more money than unemployment benefit claimants. Including them as 'incapacity benefit' claimants is misleading.

Adjustment: Exclude approximately 12% of UC incapacity caseload.

3. State Pension Age Effect

The rising State Pension Age has shifted approximately 330,000 people onto working-age benefits. This accounts for a substantial portion of the apparent caseload increase since 2008.

Adjustment: Remove the estimated SPA effect from caseload trends.

4. Baseline Year Selection

The Work Capability Assessment was newly implemented in 2010-11 and was "a complete disaster" in its early years. Starting trend analysis from 2014-15 provides a more stable comparison point.

Adjustment: Filter data to begin from 2014-15 rather than 2010-11.

Data Caveats

  • UC Health Element: DWP does not publish official element-level expenditure breakdown. Figures shown are estimates based on proportion of UC claimants with LCWRA (approx 25-30% of UC expenditure).
  • 2024-25 onwards: Figures are OBR forecasts from Autumn Budget 2024, subject to revision.
  • OECD 2023-24: These are OECD estimates from the April 2025 SOCX release.
  • Deflators: Price indices use ONS and OBR published series, rebased to 2024-25 = 100.
